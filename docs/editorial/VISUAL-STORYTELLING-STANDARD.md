# Africa Ranked — Evidence-led visual storytelling standard

Applies to all editorial article revisions, including drafts already in the CMS and those prepared by the daily three-draft workflow. A useful visual must clarify a **specific** claim, sequence, place or object. It is not a substitute for verified reporting.

## Shared reader presentation (implemented)

- Every valid, recorded key fact is presented as its **own linked fact card**; the fact text itself links to the original HTTPS supporting reference. A redundant Source column and repeated domain labels are not displayed.
- Actual verification dates are retained beside facts. A valid URL is necessary for a linked fact. URLs using unsafe schemes are never made clickable.
- The publisher, document title, HTTPS URL and actual access date remain recorded privately in the CMS editorial evidence and are accessible to readers in a **collapsed “Research references and access dates” disclosure**. This preserves fact-checkability without a long default-visible bibliography.
- These are **rendering changes**, not per-article content rewrites, so they apply to all existing and future articles that use the shared evidence modules, including private previews.
- Keep **The short answer** immediately before the article reading flow; place **Key facts** after the complete article body and FAQs, immediately before the collapsed research references, so readers can finish the on-site story before encountering outbound fact links. Public articles and private staff previews follow the same order.
- Dates are not auto-updated on page views. A verified date must describe a real check.

## Visual rhythm for 1,300+ word articles

**Required foundation:** one distinctive, relevant cover image with accurate alt text and a documented right to use it. Avoid an overlaid article-title graphic that repeats the H1 and avoid placing near-identical generated variants in the body.

**Recommended when genuinely useful:** one to three supporting images spread across different H2 sections. Use a close-up of an object, a documented scene, a useful map, a photograph of a process, or another visual that changes what a reader understands. Use the CMS **Add photo → Insert into article** controls and review the resulting private preview. Each has descriptive alt text, a factual caption, creator credit and permission/licence records. Do not reuse another article’s hero by default.

**Optional data visual:** one short infographic, comparison card, illustrated chronology, labeled process diagram or evidence-led timeline only when the content actually contains comparable numbers, named stages or verified dates. Include source(s), scope, units and measurement or publication date in the graphic itself or caption. Ensure a useful text equivalent and no unsupported numerical precision or causal claims. Never imply illustration is documentary photography. Do not invent comparative scores, charts, dates, scene photographs, people or locations.

## Suitable treatments for the three in-review research manuscripts

- **#50 — Nollywood:** a licensed, accurately identified production/distribution image; if dates are independently confirmed, a 1990s home-video → cinema → streaming *qualitative* timeline. Do not present historical output estimates as current statistics.
- **#51 — Traditional African Instruments:** distinct, documented photographs of actual mbira, kora and balafon objects, each with museum inventory information and usage permissions; optionally a labeled instrument anatomy visual when supported. Do not treat instruments from different regions as interchangeable.
- **#52 — African Fabrics and Prints:** credited museum textile-detail images and a comparison of *technique* (woven, wax-printed, printed rectangular cloth). Do not present generic textile stock as a specific community’s cloth, or assume museum listing means reuse rights.

## Guardrails and handoff

1. Research and verify source claims first; avoid generic AI-generated illustration for factual objects where faithful photos are required.
2. Use the existing article image composer, which supports first-party images, captions, source credits, alt text and permission checks.
3. Use visual modules **only where** there is an accurate licensed asset or real measurable source data. Missing suitable supporting imagery is a documented editorial follow-up, not permission to fabricate or delay all useful drafts.
4. Save and preview **a private CMS revision**; check image size, mobile cropping, factual captions, repeated artworks, keyboard reachability and reading order before moving to In Review. Existing publication and author-review gates remain unchanged.
5. Keep real source publisher/URL/access-date and fact/verified-date records even when their presentation is collapsed. A public fact link does not replace private evidence retention.

This standard is editorial guidance; it does not assert that newly licensed inner images or custom infographics have already been added to every published or draft article.
