# Open SEO research for African Ranked

Read AGENTS.md, `.agents/SEO-POLICY.md`, `.agents/product-marketing.md`, and the focused `content-strategy`/`seo-audit` adapter. The owner confirmed ads + affiliate revenue and global English-speaking readers. Keep all work in preview.

1. Discover the actual Open SEO tools and schemas available in the current session. Use the documented tools; do not invent endpoint names, metrics, credentials, or provider identity. Native tools remain unavailable here, but the application's server-side OpenSeoMcp client is verified and has a narrow research allowlist using the encrypted saved key. Reuse that client, account/project and cached research rather than asking for a key or re-buying discovery. See docs/SEO-RESEARCH-2026-11-02.md and private tool schemas. Official public workflow files remain pinned upstream references.
2. Use read-only research functions. Check whether an action consumes paid credits or changes account data; stay within the user's research request and do not start a subscription or purchase credits. No campaigns, external publishing, indexing submissions, or messages are authorized.
3. Start with `content/research/keyword-seeds.json`, existing slugs, and the owner-confirmed business context. Get actual keyword suggestions, available demand/difficulty/CPC data, search intent, related questions, SERP features, and representative competitors where the tool supports them. Record source, query, locale, date, device, units, and tool output. A missing metric stays null.
4. Compare SERP competitors by their real pages, intent, scope, complete rankings, evidence, freshness, headings, internal links, author credibility, and commercial model. A SERP competitor is not necessarily a business competitor. Do not infer authority/backlinks from design or invent competitor metrics.
5. Build a query-to-URL map. Fix or strengthen an existing page before generating a competing URL. Treat travel/safari affiliate fit as a hypothesis until research supports it; do not ignore the other categories solely because travel is easy to monetize.
6. Prioritize using documented observations: audience fit, attainable intent coverage, evidence availability, content effort, useful internal-link role, and relevant affiliate options. Do not manufacture search volume or assert a numerical score/forecast without a defined method and data.
7. Generate draft articles only after the brief identifies the query, intent, competing content, target URL, sources, actual reviewer/byline needs, and factual gaps. Keep drafts out of the published catalog until reviewed. Do not fabricate firsthand experience, authors, prices, statistics, quotations, product availability, affiliate relationships, or complete top-N rankings.
8. Make direct answers, comparison criteria, practical caveats, and relevant FAQs visible. Suggest affiliate placements only where useful, label commercial relationships when real, and use appropriate sponsored-link attributes when links actually exist. Avoid ad density/interstitial changes that harm reading.
9. Validate JSON/HTML, source/claim parity, internal links, canonical policy, schema truthfulness, and browser behavior when implementing changes. Report actual checks and leave unavailable research explicit. No ranking, citation, traffic, or ROI guarantee.
